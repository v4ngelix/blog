I love swimming and snorkeling specifically.
That's an important reason for me to leave my home country for every winter. 
One amazing place where swimming is particulary fullfilling is Sardinia in Italy. 
I visited the island some years ago and immidiatelly fell in love the local nature and vowed to one day return. 
This year I had the pleasure of returning and again, to enojy the pristine beaches and cristal clear water the island's coast offers.

The underwater enivronment is incredible.
There is a wonderful combination of patches of sand, cliffs, rocks, crevices, underwater cliffs, pebbles.
This all contributes to diverse set of submarine flora and fauna.
Seeing so many unknown fish and plants I naturally had the urge to indentify all of them.

At first I just tried to remember what I saw. 
But back at the computer I quickly realized that I couldn't recall the subjects well enought to identify any of them.
Even if I managed to find a species which kinda was similar to what I had seen, I couldnt be absolutely sure.
For example, many fish species look quite similar to each other.
The best way to differentiate one from the other would be to have a clear picture of the subject and look up what is the exact color of its head, the shape of its fins, pattern on his body. 

Fortunately, I remembered, that at the bottom of my electronics bag was an old GoPRO Hero 7, which had sit idle for years and was incidentally waterproof.
I found a piece of old cord from my campsite and made myself a bracelet for attaching the camera to myself.
This way it wouldn't get lost while diving and I wouldn't need to hold on to it the whole time while swimming. 
With this setup I hit the beach and managed to shoot a lot of photos underwater.
But back at the acommodation I saw that many of the pictures were fuzzy, missing, unclear or that some of the photos were actually videos.
That last thing annoyed me a lot. 
I would select the right mode when surfaced and while I dived, for my frustration, I quite often saw that it had decided on its own to switch to the timelaps mode or navigate into the settings menu.
I wish there would be a way to lock out the touchscreen with some kind of a key press combination - as it ruined a lot of shots for me.

With the pictures i had, I still had the problem of how to identify the fish. 
I'm not a marine biologist or ichtolog, so there was no one clear way of doing it for me.
I've tried Wikipedia. Its has creat information about marine life. 
But, well you kinda need to know what to search for - Its not good for casual identification.
I've tried to find an authoritative source for the species, but I haven't find a good material for that.
I've even asked around many books stores, in different mediterranean countries for abook on mediterranean marine life - without any success.  
I just found FROG, but I havent learned how to use it successfully yet.
There are some enthusiast made pages for identifying i.e. crabs from the east coast of USA, but not very useful for me.
I used Claude and ChatGPt about a year ago to identify a dead crab from one certain Cretan beach and well and I was quite unsucessful at the time. 
Even though I attached to the prompt very good pictures from several angles and described importany features of the animal,
, It kept recommending the same two species, mostly quite different from the one i had found.

This time it was different.
I used Claude Opus 5 and attached one or several of my underwater pictures, described the behaviour of the fish, where i found it, what it look like and i think i mostly got correct identifications.
when checking the latin names up on inaturalist, frog and wikipedia, i could confirm that LLM had identified the fish correctly.

A really small but importan thing i did to improve the flow and results of my promts was to create a project within Claude and to define:
- What will be the goal of the prompts within this project - to identify marine life.
Some background information: what is the diving location and the current season
- Also defined a procedure that when usure, ask, when need extra info, ask, i might know it but havent realized to add it.
As a result the identification really sped up and became more enjoyable.

Now i had a growing list of identified marine life. 
What to do with it next?
At first i just renamed the photos with the correct latin names.
But I had a growing wished for a nice visual way of collectin my growin list of species and somehow attach a group of realted additional information links to them.
Naturally, as an engineer, I vibecoded a webpage.

The pinterest like tiles are created from photos.'
the links to inaturalist, wikipedia and frog are auto-generated.
i added a feature to override auto-generated links, for cases where the automated links fall short.

The finished pages became a great conversation started at the campsite where i stayed.
There is a lot of AI slop in the internet these days, but the upside is that its really easy for hobby naturalist to identify unknow speciaes and to build tools to really learn about their interest subjects and to shere their finds with the world.
